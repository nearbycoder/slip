How it works

Three taps. Zero setup.

01

Open Slip on both devices

Join the same Wi-Fi network and they'll find each other within a second or two.

02

Pick what to send

Choose files, a folder, or type a message, then tap the device you want to send it to.

03

Accept on the other side

The receiver sees who's sending what, taps Accept, and the files land in their Downloads.

Compatibility

Speaks LocalSend, fluently.

Slip implements the open LocalSend protocol v2. Send to and receive from anyone running LocalSend, as well as other Slip devices, whichever app your friends prefer.

  • Multicast discoveryAnnounces on 224.0.0.167:53317, with an HTTP subnet scan as fallback.
  • REST over HTTPSSelf-signed TLS, with device fingerprints taken from each certificate's SHA-256.
  • Open and testedEvery Slip client is tested against every other, in both directions, over HTTPS and HTTP.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Something else on your mind? Open an issue on GitHub.

Is Slip really free?

Yes. Slip is free and open source under the MIT license, with no in-app purchases, subscriptions or ads.

Does it work with LocalSend?

Yes. Slip speaks the LocalSend v2 protocol, so Slip and LocalSend devices discover each other and exchange files in both directions.

Do both devices need to be on the same network?

Yes. Slip works over your local network, whether that's home Wi-Fi, an office LAN, or a phone hotspot. Nothing is routed through the internet.

Is it secure?

Transfers are encrypted with TLS by default. Each device generates its own certificate and is identified by that certificate's fingerprint. You can also require a PIN, and nothing is saved unless you accept it (or turn on Quick Save).

Why build five separate native apps?

Because they feel better. Each Slip app uses its platform's own toolkit (SwiftUI, Jetpack Compose, WinUI 3 and GTK 4), so it gets native controls, accessibility, notifications and file pickers for free, and it looks the way an app on your device should.

Why doesn't my iPhone find devices right away?

iOS asks for permission to access your local network the first time Slip runs. Tap Allow. If it was declined, turn it back on in Settings › Privacy & Security › Local Network.
